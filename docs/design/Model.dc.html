Fine-tune this print

The settings people change most often, explained in plain words. Not sure? Leave them as they are: the preset is already a good default.

{{changedLabel}}
Infill density Strength How full the inside of the object is. Prints are not solid: inside there is a light mesh. 0% is hollow, 100% is solid plastic. 10 to 20% is plenty for decoration. Use 30 to 50% for parts that hold weight or get squeezed. Higher means slower and more filament.
Infill pattern Strength The shape of that inner mesh. You never see it once printed, but it changes how strong the part is and how fast it prints. {{patternHint}}
Wall loops Strength How many lines make the outer skin of the object. More walls give a tougher shell. 2 is fine for most prints. Use 3 or 4 for parts that get screwed, clipped or dropped. This adds more strength than raising infill.
Supports Print success Temporary scaffolding printed under parts that hang in the air, like an outstretched arm. You snap it off after printing. {{supportHint}}
Brim Print success A thin flat border printed around the bottom of the object so it stays stuck to the plate. You peel it off afterwards. Helps tall, thin or tiny objects that could come loose or tip over during printing.
Ironing Looks After the last layer, the hot nozzle passes again over flat top surfaces, like an iron on a shirt. The top comes out smooth and shiny. Only useful when the model has flat tops, like a box lid or a sign. Adds about 10 to 30% print time.
Adaptive layer height Looks The printer stacks thin layers on curved or sloped areas, where steps would show, and thicker layers on straight walls. Smoother curves without slowing the whole print. Great for figurines and round shapes, little gain on boxy parts.
Seam position Looks Each layer starts and ends at one point, which leaves a tiny visible bump called the seam. This chooses where those bumps go. {{seamHint}}
